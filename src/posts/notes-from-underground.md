### Fyodor Dostoevsky
# Notes From Underground
*Translated by Richard Pevear and Larissa Volokhonsky*

---

Here are some impressions and thoughts I had while reading,

The underground man comes across as comically pathetic but also a little bit
unnerving. Commical because he is laughably awkward and maladapted to life, but
unnerving because his reasoning and commentary makes, at least to me, an
appealing case for nihilism. Throughout the text but especially in
*Underground*, the way he engages with his imagined audience of "sirs" and
"gentlement" brings this tension into focus. I don't have an answer to ease
this tension. I think underground man embodies the misery of unsatisfied
longings for life with love and purpose. It's a tragic story, but it is not
more real than Raskolnikov's.
